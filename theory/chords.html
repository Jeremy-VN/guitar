Building Major & Minor Chords

How every basic chord is manufactured from a root note and a fixed pattern of steps

A basic chord (a triad) is just three notes: the 1st, 3rd and 5th notes of a scale. So if you can build the scale from a root note, you can build the chord — you just keep every second note.

A whole step (W) = 2 frets (2 semitones); a half step (H) = 1 fret (1 semitone).

1. The two formulas

Major

Major scale, from the root: W W H W W W H

Root —W— 2 —W— 3 —H— 4 —W— 5 —W— 6 —W— 7 —H— (Root) └──────── the major CHORD = 1 · 3 · 5 ────────┘

Keep notes 1, 3, 5 and you have the major triad.

Minor

Natural-minor scale, from the root: W H W W H W W

Root —W— 2 —H— 3 —W— 4 —W— 5 —H— 6 —W— 7 —W— (Root) └──────── the minor CHORD = 1 · 3 · 5 ────────┘

Keep notes 1, 3, 5 and you have the minor triad.

2. The shortcut — stack two thirds

You don't have to spell the whole scale. Counting only in semitones (frets) from the root:

ChordRoot → 3rd3rd → 5thRoot → 5th
Major triad4 semitones (major 3rd)3 semitones (minor 3rd)7 (perfect 5th)
Minor triad3 semitones (minor 3rd)4 semitones (major 3rd)7 (perfect 5th)

So major = 4 then 3, minor = 3 then 4. The 5th is a perfect 5th (7 frets) either way.

The one‑note difference. The root and the 5th are identical in both. Only the 3rd moves — a semitone lower for minor. A major = A C# E; A minor = A C E. Flatten the middle note and a major chord becomes minor.

3. Every natural root, A–G

The three notes of each chord (root · 3rd · 5th):

RootMajor chordMinor chord
AA · C# · EA · C · E
BB · D# · F#B · D · F#
CC · E · GC · E♭ · G
DD · F# · AD · F · A
EE · G# · BE · G · B
FF · A · CF · A♭ · C
GG · B · DG · B♭ · D

In every row the root and 5th are the same across major and minor — only the yellow 3rd changes.

Take the 1st, 3rd and 5th note of any row and you get that chord in the table above. (These are the natural minor scales; the chord only needs the 1‑3‑5, which is the same whichever minor scale you use.)

5. Two quick tips

  • Name notes by letter first. A triad always uses every second letter — A‑C‑E, B‑D‑F, C‑E‑G, etc. Then add the sharp/flat needed to hit the right fret. That's why C minor's 3rd is written E♭, not D# — the letter must be E.
  • To go major → minor, drop the 3rd by one fret; minor → major, raise it one fret. Nothing else moves.
