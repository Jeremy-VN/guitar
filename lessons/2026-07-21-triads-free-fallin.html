The "four-chord" pop progression (the Taylor-Swift / half-the-charts one — that's the "T. Swift" aside in the lesson).

Major vs minor in any major key ✓ verified

  • Major: I, IV, V → D, G, A
  • minor: ii, iii, vi → Em, F#m, Bm
  • diminished: vii° → C#°

So D, A, G are major; only Bm (the vi) is minor.

A quick primer — triads & inversions (background, for learning)

What a triad is

  • A triad is a 3‑note chord — the most basic chord there is.
  • Built by stacking thirds from a root: root + 3rd + 5th. E.g. D major = D (root) + F# (3rd) + A (5th).
  • The 3rd decides major vs minor: a major 3rd (4 frets above the root) = major; a minor 3rd (3 frets) = minor. D–F#–A = D major; D–F–A = D minor.
  • The 5th (7 frets above the root) is the same either way — it just adds body.

Inversions — which note is on the bottom

  • An inversion is the same three notes reshuffled so a different one is lowest. Still the same chord — just a different voicing, colour, and top note.
  • Root position — root on the bottom: D–F#–A (D lowest).
  • 1st inversion — the 3rd on the bottom: F#–A–D (F# lowest).
  • 2nd inversion — the 5th on the bottom: A–D–F# (A lowest).
  • Careful with the term: root position is not an inversion — it's the un‑inverted chord. So a triad strictly has two inversions (1st and 2nd), giving three arrangements in total (root position + those two). Three notes → three possible bass notes → three positions. (Guitarists often say "three inversions" loosely — handy to know the exact count.)

The common myth: there is no "3rd inversion" of a triad

  • A 3rd inversion only exists for a 4‑note chord (a 7th chord). E.g. D7 = D–F#–A–C; with the 7th (C) in the bass that's the 3rd inversion.
  • In the lesson the "first, second, third" shorthand really means the three triad positions (root, 1st, 2nd). Handy to know the exact terms.

Why it matters here

  • On the top 3 strings, each of D, A, Bm, G has those three positions (root + two inversions).
  • Moving the same chord up the neck cycles through them — same chord, new inversion, higher top note. That's the whole "up the neck" idea in Part 1.
  • Inversions also let you keep common tones between chords (smooth voice‑leading) — which is exactly why the drone/let‑ring trick works.
  • Slash‑chord naming (optional): inversions are often written as D/F# (D major, F# in the bass = 1st inversion) or D/A (2nd inversion).

2. Part 1 — Triads in D, up the neck (top 3 strings)

All on strings 1–2–3 (high E, B, G). Three notes on three strings = three positions (root position + two inversions). You play the whole D–A–Bm–G in three places; each move up the neck rotates you to the next one — same four chords, new voicing. Tab reads high‑E → B → G, numbers = fret.

The exercises, in order ✓ verified

  • 1. Strum the triads — quarter notes, all downstrokes. 1 2 3 4
  • 2. Arpeggiate — each note individually, eighth notes. 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &
  • 3. Let ring — hold notes so they overlap; when you reach A, the D and G are still ringing. Lush, Coldplay‑ish pad.
  • 4. Colour notes — enrich each triad (below).
  • 5. Common‑shape trick — D shape doubles as Bm (below).
  • 6. Drone a common tone — keep A (or D) sounding underneath; he noted this sits even better if you move it to strings 2‑3‑4.

Common‑shape trick (exercise 5) ✓ verified

  • Play the D triad shape over B and, with a B in the bass, it becomes Bm7 — same top notes, different function. His words: "D shape over D is D; over B minor it's B minor 7."
  • Lesson: hold one note common between neighbouring shapes and minimise hand movement — slide one finger rather than re‑form the whole grip.

Exactly how he built it, in his words:

  • D = E‑shape D voicing, index around fret 10.
  • A = drop back, barre the top 3 strings at fret 9, 2nd finger on the B string (fret 10) → position 9.
  • Bm = a D‑minor shape, one fret up so it includes the D → around fret 11.
  • G = from that Bm, push the 2nd finger out to the 12th fret (B minor at 11 → G at 12).

3. Part 2 — "Free Fallin'" (Tom Petty)

The whole song = 3 chords ✓ verified

  • Trimmed to I – V – IV = D – A – G shapes (drop the Bm).
  • Petty put a capo on the 3rd fret to sing it in F: capo 3 → D=F, A=C, G=Bb. A "couch song" — three chords, droning top, all downstrokes.

Guitar 1 — the main riff (capo 3, D‑shapes) ✓ verified

  • Top 4 strings only, all downstrokes, high D droning on the B string (3rd fret above the capo) the whole time.
  • Chords cycle D → Asus4 → G (G hit twice — the "double G"). Timing 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &, steady.

Guitar 2 — the layer (capo 1, E‑shapes) ✓ verified

  • Same three chords, higher, different drone (an E‑shape F on top). E‑shapes need only capo 1 to land in F.
  • Shapes: E (=F) → A9 (=Bb) → B (=C) — same F, Bb, C as Guitar 1, voiced up and layered. Live it's strummed lighter — a bit of "jingle‑jangle" once bass and drums are in.

Guitars 3 & 4 — power chords + solo

  • Guitar 3: power chords dragging up the neck — F → Bb — palm‑muted, bridge (back) pickup, pinky‑led to stretch/compress as it moves. Keep it calm and even.
  • Guitar 4: a simple doubled lead line that barely surfaces. ⚑ not detailed in the lesson.
▶ Reference — Guitar 3 power chords F5 · Bb5 (in F, palm-muted feel)

Capo / setup tips ✓ verified

  • Use a flat‑bottomed capo for a flatter neck. His guitars: modern Fender ≈ 9.5" radius, old‑school Fender ≈ 7.25" (curvier capo suits the 7.25").
  • Shubb‑style lever capo (no spring — you "shove" it): fit it over the strings first, then seat it against the back of the neck.
  • Set tension so strings don't buzz but don't go sharp — just firm enough to fret clean.

Lyric reference

"She's a good girl, loves her mama / loves Jesus and America too… / and I'm free — free fallin'."

4. Suggested practice order

  1. Position‑1 triads: strum D–A–Bm–G, quarter notes, all downstrokes.
  2. Same, arpeggiated in eighths.
  3. Add let‑ring / drone (keep the B‑string D ringing).
  4. Colour notes (A6, Gadd9); D‑shape‑as‑Bm7 trick.
  5. Walk it up: open → position 5 → top group (9–12).
  6. "Free Fallin'": capo 3, Guitar‑1 riff (D–Asus4–G, D‑drone, all downstrokes) until the pulse is dead even.
